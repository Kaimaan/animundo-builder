## Writing for children

The readers are young children playing as the animal. Some can't read yet, and a grown-up may read aloud. Everything you write follows these rules:

- Plain words, short sentences, present tense, warm and calm, like the examples: what the animal sees, smells and does.
- Being caught or eaten is said simply ("The fox catches a vole."). No wounds, blood, pain or suffering.
- Young animals "are born", with the real season only where the place has seasons. Nothing about mating.
- Nothing that could frighten a young child at bedtime: no threats aimed at the player, and no death described.
- No people: no humans, hunters, weapons, traps, pollution, religion, politics, war, borders or disputed places.
- No real-world advice: never tell the child to touch, eat, pick, approach or feed anything. The only real-world words allowed are the game's own, for poisonous plants: "Poisonous! Look, but don't pick." and "Never eat it."
- No brands, products, links, phone numbers or names of real people.
- Only what the sources say. If you're not sure, leave it out: a short true text is better than a long one.
- For a threatened or protected species, never name or place a real breeding, nesting, denning or resting site.
